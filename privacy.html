Privacy

Looot – the app (Android) and the web site emilevloot.github.io/vlooot. Last changed: 6 October 2026.

In short: Looot has no accounts, no ads and no analytics. The maker of the game collects no data about you. The game runs on your own device; only when you play online with friends does it connect to other players.

What stays on your device

  • The name you type for online games, a random player number (so you get your seat back after a lost connection) and whether the coach is on. They are kept in the app's own storage and never sent to the maker. Uninstalling the app (or clearing the site's data) removes them.
  • The game itself, the computer players and the coach run entirely on your device.

Playing online with friends

  • To find each other, devices use PeerJS, a free connection service. Its server sees the game code and your device's internet address while it connects you; after that the devices talk directly (WebRTC). When a direct connection is not possible, the game data may go through PeerJS's relay server. See PeerJS for how it handles this.
  • The players in your game see the name you chose and your moves. Nothing else about you is shared.
  • If you never play online, none of this happens.

The web site

The web site is hosted by GitHub Pages; GitHub may keep technical logs (such as internet addresses) to run its service. The site loads fonts from Google Fonts and code from jsDelivr, which also see your internet address. The Android app has its own copy of these files.

Children

Looot is not directed at children under 13 and collects no personal data from anyone.

Questions

Open an issue at github.com/emilevloot/vlooot/issues. If this policy changes, the new version will be on this page with a new date.

Nederlands, kort

Looot heeft geen accounts, geen advertenties en geen analytics; de maker verzamelt geen gegevens. Je naam voor online spelen, een willekeurig spelersnummer en de coach-instelling blijven op je eigen toestel. Bij online spelen met vrienden zoeken de toestellen elkaar via PeerJS (die server ziet de spelcode en je internetadres) en praten ze daarna rechtstreeks met elkaar; je medespelers zien je naam en je zetten.
